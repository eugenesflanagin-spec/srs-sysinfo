# SRS 2.2 - Power & cost, new benchmarks, and tabs your way

SRS 2.2 shows what your PC's power use costs, adds new benchmarks (among them Cache & Memory,
Storage, OS Latency and GPU tests), and lets you arrange the main window the way you like it: sort any table,
keep the tabs you want, and manage what starts with your PC. Everything from 2.0 is still there,
and the classic SIV-style windows too.

2.1 was never released on its own: this release also contains everything announced for 2.1.

## What's new

**Power & cost** (a new page right under Sensors, Alt+9)

- How much power the whole PC uses at the wall, with its likely range, and what that costs per
  hour, day, month and year at your price. The processor and graphics card are measured; the rest
  is estimated from a parts list and your power supply's efficiency.
- Recording (off, for a while, or always) into a small database on your PC, with history by
  hour, day, week or month, busy sessions such as games or renders, CSV export, alarms, and
  typical electricity prices for your area that work offline. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **These are estimates, not a meter reading and not a bill.** The page says so the first time it
  opens and on every export.

**New benchmarks** (Benchmarks & stress page, Alt+=)

- **Cache & Memory**: read, write and copy speed and latency of the RAM and the L1, L2 and L3
  caches, with the measured base clock and Infinity Fabric clock. The Linux System Audit (LSA)
  project is now part of SRS.
- **Storage**: read and write speed of a drive (sequential and 4K random, with response times),
  using only its own temporary test file, which can never be left behind.
- **OS Latency**: how quickly Linux answers a program - system calls, switching between
  programs, starting threads and programs, wake-ups, how late timers fire (with the CPU scheduler
  you use, such as scx_lavd, BORE or EEVDF), page faults and small-file operations. Light: one or
  two CPUs for about 6 seconds, and it changes nothing on your system.
- **CPU Compute**: number crunching - matrix multiply, FFT, gravity simulation, a fractal and a
  small ray tracer - on one thread and on all threads, with the fastest vector code your CPU has
  (up to AVX-512). Every answer is checked, so an unstable overclock or undervolt shows up as
  WRONG instead of a fast score.
- **Everyday Work**: the work your PC does all day - compressing and unpacking files (zlib, zstd,
  lz4 and xz, whichever your system has), sorting, checksums, searching and checking text, and
  running a small script-like program - on one thread and on all threads, every result checked.
  It uses data it makes itself, never your files.
- **Network**: how fast your PC moves network data on itself (nothing leaves the computer), and
  an optional internet speed test - name lookup, connect time, download and upload with one and
  several connections - against Cloudflare's public speed test. The internet test asks
  separately, tells you how much data it may use (up to about 1000 MB) and never runs without
  your yes. No addresses or network names are shown or saved.
- **GPU Benchmark**: how fast your graphics card does sums (single, half and double precision and
  whole numbers), how fast its memory reads, writes and copies, how fast data moves to and from it,
  and how quickly it answers - through Vulkan, with every result checked. About 15 seconds at full
  GPU load. Vulkan is used only if your system has it.
- **GPU Burnin**: a stress test for the graphics card. It keeps the GPU fully busy and checks
  every result, so a GPU overclock or undervolt that is not stable shows up as errors (0 errors =
  PASS), with the temperatures, power, clock and fan speed live. It runs through Vulkan, ROCm /
  HIP, CUDA or OpenCL - whichever you have. It asks first, stops by itself at a temperature limit
  (100 °C unless you change it) or when the driver reports a problem, and never changes clocks,
  voltages or fans.
- **GPU Compute: Vulkan vs HIP vs CUDA vs OpenCL**: the same GPU work through every way of
  programming your card that you have installed, side by side - SRS's own code and the maker's
  tuned library, the fastest marked and each compared with Vulkan. It recognises ZLUDA (CUDA on an
  AMD card) and shows every OpenCL driver separately.
- **SRS Pi** (with SuperPi's sizes too), **Crypto** (AES and SHA-256), **Prime Sieve**, and a
  **benchmark history** of your runs.
- **Every benchmark asks before it starts** and tells you what it does, how long it takes, how
  busy it makes your PC and (SRS Pi) how much memory it needs. For the heavy runs (all CPU threads
  at once, SRS Pi from 8 million digits, Prime Sieve from 10 billion) **No** is the default answer.
  The command line asks too; `--yes` starts a benchmark without the question, for scripts. Only one
  benchmark runs at a time, and CPU Burnin and GPU Burnin never run together.

**The main window, your way**

- **Sortable tables**: click a column heading (▲ then ▼). Sizes, clocks, temperatures, fan speeds
  and dates sort by value. **Reset view** puts the original order back; reports and Copy keep it.
- **Tabs and lineups**: add a tab with **+** (Ctrl+T), close, replace, drag and keep tabs; pick a
  tab bar of one scrolling row, wrapped rows or a side list. **Lineups** save the lot: Basic,
  Enthusiast and Expert (the default) are built in, and you can save, export and import your own.
  **All N items** is a searchable jump list.
- **Less clutter**: Windows-only items and hardware your PC does not have are hidden.
  **Options -> Check now** also hides items that come out empty on your PC (or turn on **Check
  daily**; it is off unless you do). The check never touches the network items.
  **Options -> Show hidden items** lists everything hidden.
- **Startup manager** (Operating system -> Startup programs): the tab opens on the read-only
  **Report**; **Manage**, one click away, switches autostart entries and services off and on
  without deleting anything, with Undo, Restore default and **Add to startup...**.
- **CPU Clocks: stock and in effect now**: the measured base clock, core base, boost and current
  clock at stock and in effect, and FCLK, UCLK and MCLK from the SMU.
- **Full access / Limited badge** in the title bar, with a **Run as root...** button when root data
  is missing.

With Power & cost under Sensors, the page keys follow the sidebar: USB & PCI devices is now
Alt+0, Operating system Alt+-, Benchmarks & stress Alt+= and Tools Alt+Backspace.

Existing commands, options, item IDs and report formats are unchanged; the CPU Clocks item gains
its new table in reports too. New commands: `sysreport-search cachemem`, `storage`,
`os-latency`, `compute`, `work`, `net`, `gpu`, `gpu-burn`, `gpu-compare`, `pi`, `crypto` and `prime`. Settings carry over from 2.0.

The user guide has new sections on Power & cost, the new benchmarks, arranging tabs and lineups,
sortable tables, the Startup manager and the badge. [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md) lists the public
data behind the price and CO2 tables and their licences.

Full list in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).

## Tested on one PC (and what that means)

I build and test SRS on a single machine: a Ryzen 7 7800X3D with a Radeon RX 9070 XT, on a wired
connection. I've done everything I can for the rest (checks that don't need the hardware, careful
reading of the docs, test builds), but I can't try what I don't own. So anything that needs other
hardware, such as NVIDIA CUDA (and ZLUDA), Intel graphics, ROCm/HIP or OpenCL setups other than
mine, Wi-Fi or VPN network tests, or ARM and 32-bit builds, may have a bug or two I haven't met yet. If you find one, please tell me: open an issue on
[GitHub](https://github.com/eugenesflanagin-spec/srs-sysinfo/issues) with your hardware and the
report SRS made, and I'll fix it.

Not tried on real hardware so far:

<!-- untested areas: each benchmark adds its line; the GPU pass adds the GPU lines here -->
- GPU benchmarks: Vulkan only on a software GPU (lavapipe, which runs on the CPU), never on a
  real graphics card; ROCm/HIP, CUDA, ZLUDA and OpenCL never run on real hardware (only checked
  that their libraries load and have every function SRS needs); the temperature, power, clock
  and fan read-outs for NVIDIA and Intel graphics are untested.
- ARM (aarch64) and 32-bit (i686) builds: compile-checked only.
- OS Latency: run only with the scx_lavd scheduler; BORE, EEVDF and CFS are recognised but were
  not run here.
- CPU Compute: the AVX-512 code has only run on a Zen 4 CPU; the SSE2 and AVX2 code was checked by
  forcing it on that same CPU, not on older CPUs.
- Everyday Work: run with the compression libraries of one Arch Linux system; other versions
  should work but give different speeds.
- Network: run on one wired Ethernet connection only; Wi-Fi, VPN and metered or mobile
  connections are untested. The internet speed test was run once on that connection, against
  Cloudflare, with sensible results (download several hundred Mbit/s, upload about 60 Mbit/s), and
  the report showed no IP address, host name or MAC address.

**GPU features and the hardware I can test them on**

I build and test SRS on a single machine: a Ryzen 7 7800X3D with a Radeon RX 9070 XT. I can't try
what I don't own, so GPU features for other hardware, such as NVIDIA CUDA (and ZLUDA), Intel
graphics, or any ROCm/HIP and OpenCL setup other than mine, may have a bug or two I haven't met
yet. If you find one, please open an issue on GitHub with your GPU and the report SRS made, and
I'll fix it.

**What has been tried, and what has not**

- Tried: the Vulkan tests, GPU Burnin and the GPU Compute comparison were run on lavapipe (the
  Mesa software renderer), with small sizes, so the logic, the error counting, Stop and the result
  pages are checked. The Vulkan type layouts were checked against the official Khronos header for
  64-bit and 32-bit builds.
- Tried only in part: ROCm/HIP, CUDA and OpenCL. I checked that the libraries load and that every
  function SRS needs is found in them (including on my own ROCm, my ZLUDA and my OpenCL loader),
  and that a missing library or an OpenCL loader with no driver is handled cleanly. I did **not**
  run any HIP, CUDA or OpenCL work on a GPU from the tests.
- Not run on real hardware: Vulkan on a real GPU, GPU Burnin on a real GPU, CUDA on NVIDIA, ZLUDA,
  ROCm/HIP on AMD, OpenCL on AMD (ROCm) and Mesa Rusticl, anything on Intel graphics, and the
  temperature, power, clock and fan read-outs for NVIDIA and Intel.

**Things to know**

- A back end that is not installed shows as "not installed" with a one-line hint (for example
  "install ROCm"); the others still run.
- If your CUDA library is really ZLUDA (CUDA calls translated to an AMD GPU), the column is
  labelled "CUDA (ZLUDA)".
- Every OpenCL driver is its own column, for example "OpenCL (ROCm)" and "OpenCL (Rusticl)" on the
  same GPU; CPU drivers (PoCL) are marked CPU.
- Speeds are only comparable between alike SRS builds and the same GPU driver / runtime versions.
- GPU Burnin ends by itself on a driver error, a GPU that stops answering, a piece of work that
  takes far too long, or a temperature at the limit (default 100 °C). It never changes clocks,
  voltages or fans. It asks before it starts.

## Install

**Arch Linux**

```sh
sudo pacman -U srs-sysinfo-2.2.0-1-x86_64.pkg.tar.zst
# or from the AUR:  yay -S srs-sysinfo
```

**Debian / Ubuntu**

```sh
sudo apt install ./srs-sysinfo_2.2.0-1.deb13_amd64.deb        # or the .ubuntu24.04 file
```

**Fedora / openSUSE**

```sh
sudo dnf install ./srs-sysinfo-2.2.0-1.fc44.x86_64.rpm
sudo zypper install ./srs-sysinfo-2.2.0-1.tw.x86_64.rpm
```

**Any other distribution**

```sh
tar xf srs-sysinfo-2.2.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz
cd srs-sysinfo-2.2.0-linux-x86_64
./usr/bin/srs-gui                     # run in place, or install:
sudo cp -r usr/bin usr/share /usr/local/
```

`INSTALL.txt` in the tarball covers the optional boot-time service and uninstalling.

**AppImage (any distribution)**

```sh
chmod +x srs-sysinfo-2.2.0-x86_64.AppImage
./srs-sysinfo-2.2.0-x86_64.AppImage
```

With arguments it is the command line (`--version`, `find ...`, `srs-sysreport`). Run as root
works from it; the boot-time refresh needs the package for your distro. AppImageUpdate (or a
manager that uses it, such as AppImageLauncher) keeps it up to date.

## Requirements

- 64-bit x86 Linux. Use the package for your distro; the generic `linux-x86_64.tar.gz` is built from the
  Ubuntu 24.04 package and needs glibc 2.38 or newer (check with `ldd --version`)
- GTK 3, GLib, Pango, Cairo, SQLite 3 (for Power & cost), and `hwdata`
- Optional, for more detail: polkit (for `srs-root`), dmidecode, smartmontools, nvme-cli, i2c-tools,
  pciutils, usbutils, lm-sensors, vulkan-tools, clinfo, mesa-utils, libayatana-appindicator (tray icon);
  zlib, zstd, lz4 and xz (liblzma) for the Everyday Work benchmark's compression rows (almost every
  system has them); libcurl for the Network benchmark's internet speed test; the Vulkan loader and
  your GPU's Vulkan driver for the GPU benchmarks; ROCm (HIP), CUDA or an OpenCL driver (with
  CLBlast) only if you want those back ends in GPU Burnin and the GPU Compute comparison
- For guide links at the exact page: Okular, Evince or Papers (other viewers open the HTML guide)

## Upgrading from 2.0

Your settings carry over, and so do your tabs: the first time you start 2.2, SRS asks which tab
lineup you want (Basic, Enthusiast, which is your current 2.0 tabs and is preselected, or Expert)
and tells you where to change it later: the menu button, then Lineup. If you liked 2.0's plain-text tables, untick **Options -> Sortable tables**.
For Power & cost, open the page once and check **Setup**: pick your power supply and add the parts
SRS cannot see (extra fans, a pump, RGB), so the estimate fits your PC.
